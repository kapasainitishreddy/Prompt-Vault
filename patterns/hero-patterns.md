# Hero lab: 12 different visual grammars

A hero is not a mandatory centered headline over a gradient. Pick a grammar because it communicates the **product** and serves a **user's next action**. Always keep headline, CTA, readable structure and useful proof.

| # | Composition | Best for | Signature device | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Editorial masthead** | Portfolio, journal, books, cultural work | Type-led asymmetric hierarchy, intentional whitespace, rule lines, marginalia | Decorative serif everywhere; unclear CTA |
| 2 | **Product on a stage** | Physical products, hardware, detailed UI | One sharp high-fidelity product visual as the focal object | Floating unreadable screenshots, fake 3D |
| 3 | **Live demo split** | Developer tool, interactive SaaS | Clear copy on one side; real functioning mini-demo on the other | Fake input fields or non-functional buttons |
| 4 | **Metric storytelling** | Analytics, fintech, operational software | Honest, labeled chart/detail showing a real problem solved | Invented metrics, financial deception |
| 5 | **Cinematic chapter** | Film, game, interactive narrative | Meaningful still/sequence with a legible overlaid title | Autoplay sound, contrast lost in imagery |
| 6 | **Catalog gallery** | Commerce, collection, editorial archive | Curated, intentionally sized cards with strong cropping | Everything same size; generic marketplace filler |
| 7 | **Blueprint / schematic** | Infrastructure, developer tools, engineering | Precise system illustration, labeled pathways | Fake technical diagrams, tiny labels |
| 8 | **Typographic manifesto** | Campaign, purpose-driven collective | One sharply written statement with unexpected type scale | Empty inspirational slogans |
| 9 | **Before/after proof** | Restoration, optimization, workflow transformations | Accessible annotated comparison users can inspect | Misleading manipulation or hidden controls |
| 10 | **Workbench / canvas** | Creative tools, builders, agents | The product interface **is** the hero; interactive affordance | Dashboard screenshot without narrative |
| 11 | **Spatial field** | Maps, events, travel, geospatial tools | Real data pins/routes/contours, with story and accessible list alternative | Animated particles unrelated to the product |
| 12 | **Human documentary** | Services, community, nonprofit | Consent-cleared authentic image + human language | Stock-photo clichés and invented testimonials |

## Design DNA recipe

Fill all of these *before* choosing components:

- **Identity:** who, one-sentence promise, tone in three *contrasting* words.
- **Opposition:** three clichés from this category that will be deliberately avoided.
- **Composition:** one grammar from the atlas, one original structural twist.
- **Typographic roles:** display, headings, body, data and label; sizes and line lengths.
- **Color strategy:** 2–4 brand colors plus neutrals and semantic statuses; contrast measured.
- **Image logic:** original product screenshot / licensed photo / SVG diagram / no image.
- **Motion contract:** why each animation exists, duration, trigger, reduced-motion alternative.
- **Trust:** only verifiable proof, names, figures, integrations and customer quotes.
- **Responsiveness:** reflow to 360px, 390px, 768px, 1024px, 1440px; no clipped claims or hidden primary CTA.
- **Action:** primary next step and confirmation it really works.

## Variant experiment

Explore **three structurally different** candidate directions before implementing one. Recoloring the same layout does not count. For each direction provide: a 70-word concept, ASCII wireframe, typography rationale, audience fit, likely accessibility problems, cost and originality risk. Choose the most purposeful, not the most ornamented.

## Hero review gates

1. A first-time visitor can tell **what it is**, **who it serves**, and **what to do next** without reading six paragraphs.
2. The product's **actual differentiator** is visible in copy, visual, or interaction.
3. Primary action is visible, specific, accessible, and works; the secondary CTA is genuinely secondary.
4. Photo/illustration/UI imagery is high-resolution, readable and legally usable. Real screen contents are not fabricated.
5. Screenshot checks: desktop **1440×900**, tablet **768×1024**, mobile **390×844** and **360×800**.
6. Navigation, skip link, focus, zoom, text wrapping, image alt, motion preferences and performance are checked.
7. When the hero is a product demo, input validation, loading, error and empty states must be demonstrated.

### Anti-slop checklist

Reject a design if any of these appear **without an explicit product-based justification**: purple glow blobs, indistinguishable three-card feature rows, nine over-rounded boxes, gradient text everywhere, glassmorphism as default, meaningless stat counters, generic "Unlock your potential" copy, logos of imaginary clients, synthetic testimonials, an animated hero covering the CTA, one centered template used for every sector.

These are **warning signs, not universal bans**. Real brands can legitimately use purple, cards and gradients. The critique must show *why* this implementation helps or hurts this user's job, not enforce an arbitrary fashion.
